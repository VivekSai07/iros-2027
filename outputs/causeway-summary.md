## Key Claims
- **Task islands exist.** A VLA that completes task B reliably from its standard start can fail to start B from states left behind by task A. Accessibility depends on physical state relative to B, not on elapsed time. One example is non-monotone: along source task A3, |F(s)| goes 10 → 2 → 4. [V]
- **The "gate".** Swapping the instruction still changes the text-token representation, but its effect on the decoded action drops by **1–2 orders of magnitude** before contact on all three architectures. A state-directed gradient can still steer the action-stream representation at those states. [V]
- **Causeway is training-free.** It back-propagates a pose-error objective (position, approach axis, roll, gripper channels) through the frozen decoder. It adds a shared perturbation δ to the action-token hidden states, and the frozen VLA decodes every action. Engagement and handoff are scheduled from proprioception. [V]
- **LIBERO-Goal results (71 cross-object pairs).** Bare-switch success averages **26.0% (π0.5), 3.4% (OFT) and 9.9% (GR00T)**. Causeway raises these to **61.6%, 64.5% and 47.5%**. Gains are positive for every model, timing and target, from +28.6 pp (GR00T, after completion) to +72.1 pp (OFT, step 30). Reach improves by 42–72 pp. [V]
- **Baselines.** Prompt or activation contrast does not help: CPG adds at most +3.6 pp, and CAA is at or below the bare switch on average. Action-space (Cartesian residual) steering on π0.5 helps only at step 30. On OFT, Causeway (64.5%) beats an externally controlled single-point servo (47.2%) but falls below a wrist-first servo (73.7%) and direct state placement (79.2%). [V]
- **Generalisation.** On LIBERO-Object, Causeway raises the committed-state switch from 0% to 57.5% on the evaluated pairs. The real xArm results are in the table above. [V]

## Field Context
- The paper positions itself next to **ReSteer** (state-dependent steerability, fixed by generated transition data plus self-refining BC) and **SwitchVLA** (trained forward/rollback/advance behaviours). It casts itself as the complementary case: *online, training-free* recovery after a bare switch has already failed. [V]
- It contrasts with inference-time guidance (CFG/contrastive decoding, **DeLock** CPG arXiv:2604.23121) and activation steering (ActAdd, CAA, RepE), which amplify the instruction contrast. Causeway instead targets *state recovery*. It also contrasts with external recovery (servoing, **Recovery Chaining**), placing itself "between language-only guidance and external control". It cites the initiation-set view of options and skill chaining. [V]
- It cites "Yell at your robot" (language corrections) but does not evaluate speech or human-issued corrections. [V]
- I have not checked externally how this relates to LIBERO-MAX, ARMS or SocialVLA. Causeway does not cite them. [V absence in the reference list]

## Technical Hinges
1. **An accessibility diagnosis instead of a generic failure label.** The escape map, the executable set F(s) = {ℓ : ξ_ℓ(s) ∈ M_ℓ} and the measured entry-basin tolerances (≈30 mm position, ≈±20° approach axis, asymmetric roll where −0.15 rad costs 20 pp; 10 tasks × 4 trials per point) together turn "the switch failed" into "the state is outside B's demonstrated basin". The contrast with ReSteer is that it measures steerability, while Causeway localises *why* and *where*. [V]
2. **Representation-level, state-directed write.** This is the core finding of H3: you need both state-directed steering and representation-level injection, because instruction-contrast directions (CAA) and action-space residuals each fail. [V]
3. **A schedule with a phase-conditioned target.** The gripper-open branch and the closed-gripper branch of S_B(g) allow re-entry to the right phase, such as rerouting a held object without regrasping. A proprioceptive handoff returns control to the plain policy. [V]

## Methodology From Primitives
- **Setup.** Run a policy on instruction ℓA from LIBERO initial states, then *permanently* replace the instruction with ℓB at timing τ ∈ {step 30, step 60, done}. Success is B's LIBERO goal predicate. Because the switch state is random, results are averaged over the switch-state distribution of A. [V]
- **Reference levels.** From-scratch ρ_B, which is near-perfect for all models in sim [V]. On OFT there are also privileged references: state placement and two servos [V].
- **Re-entry target T\*.** T* is extracted from B's demonstrations by one fixed rule, with no pair-specific tuning. Gains are a single profile for OFT and GR00T and three geometry-driven profiles for π0.5. The schedule thresholds γ = 0.3 rad, Nγ = 20 and Nd = 15 are shared across models. [V]
- **Compute cost (A100-80GB).** Per-chunk latency rises 2.5–2.8×: OFT 102 → 253 ms, π0.5 75 → 187 ms, GR00T 128 → 362 ms. That gives 4.0/5.3/2.8 Hz replanning and up to +4.3 GiB peak memory, but only during re-entry. [V] *Inference:* this needs white-box backprop through the decoder. For SmolVLA (a flow-matching action expert) this looks feasible in principle. ACT has no language input and no instruction switch, so Causeway does not apply to it directly. [inference]
- **Evidence type.** Large-sample simulation (5,112 trials per model) plus a small real-robot study (5 trials per cell, 210 real trials across bare and Causeway). I computed the 210 from the table layout; the paper does not state it. [V for the cells; the total is my arithmetic]
- **Failure modes named.** Stalling, continuing toward the source object, the hand ending inside the drawer (A3), and the gripper left closed after completion (A7). [V]

## Limitations
- Stated by the paper: it depends on task-relative coordinates ξ and on demonstration coverage. It degrades under unmodeled factors (large scene shifts, in-grasp dynamics) and needs per-architecture calibration (write layers and gains). [V]
- Observed in the text: object orientations are fixed in the evaluation settings, which is why orientation is kept in the world frame. Five LIBERO-Goal pairs are excluded. The real study has 5 trials per cell and a single robot and policy. π0.5 uses three gain profiles. [V]
- Absent from the paper, and relevant to us: no speech or audio, no humans issuing instructions, no overlap between an utterance and motion (the swap is instantaneous text), no residual-motion, retraction, spurious-halt or redirect-latency metrics, no non-permanent interruptions (backchannel, cancel-then-resume), and no human validation of the metrics. ## What we could reuse for a speech-overlap interruption benchmark (SO-101, SmolVLA/ACT)
All items in this section are *inference / recommendation*. They are not claims made by the paper.
1. **The timing grid.** Use Causeway's three onsets (approach, pre-grasp/held, post-completion) as our phase anchors and extend them to approach / grasp / transport / place. Define onsets by **hand–object distance or gripper state**, as the escape map does (250/180 mm, pre-grasp), rather than by step count, because Causeway shows that accessibility follows state, not time. For speech, anchor **utterance onset** and record utterance end separately, so that the overlap duration becomes a controlled variable.
2. **The success definition and normalisation.** Adopt ρ_{A→B}^{(τ)} averaged over switch states and report it **relative to solo ρ_B** (Table II reports "Solo"). Add a "bare switch" condition as the lower bound. For our benchmark the bare switch is text injection of the transcript at onset, which gives a text-vs-speech paired arm on the same states.
3. **Reach and Fin|R decomposition.** Split the outcome into "did the arm get back to a state where B is executable?" and "did it finish B from there?". This maps directly onto our repair metrics (post-repair success vs. re-entry). Escape rate and |F(s)| would give a cheap state-level diagnostic of which onsets are inherently hard on SO-101.
4. **The ordered-pair design with same-object vs. cross-object pairs.** These two strata behave differently (same-object switches survive while the holding states overlap), and our taxonomy should stratify by them.
5. **The failure vocabulary.** Causeway's "stalling" and "continuing toward the source object" are concrete precedents for our "spurious halt" and "contextual inertia / wrong-object commit" categories. We should cite them as observed failure modes, not as formal metrics.
6. **What we cannot reuse.** No released code was found, so we cannot reuse an evaluation harness. The method needs gradient access to a VLA decoder, which might work as a repair baseline on SmolVLA and is not applicable to ACT. Our benchmark must add what Causeway lacks: audio and overlap, non-permanent interruption types, motion-quality metrics (residual motion, retraction), and human validation.

## Follow-up Questions
1. On SO-101 with SmolVLA, how large are bare-switch task islands at Causeway-style onsets? If text-only bare-switch success is already near 0% at pre-grasp, speech-specific effects may be swamped by the island effect. We would then need to separate "ASR/overlap failure" from "state inaccessibility", for example with a placement reference as Causeway does.
2. Does the "gate" (‖Δa‖ attenuation before contact) also appear in SmolVLA? It is cheap to measure with two forward passes. If it does, any spoken correction issued close to contact will fail regardless of speech handling, and that changes which onsets the benchmark should emphasise.
3. Is the with/without-event paired protocol really LIBERO-MAX's rather than Causeway's? The novelty note (§0 and §5, rank 2) should be corrected after reading LIBERO-MAX's full text.

## Verdict
Causeway is a careful, internally consistent method paper. Every Table I and II average I recomputed matches the reported values. It establishes that instruction switching in frozen VLAs fails mainly because of *state accessibility* rather than language understanding, and that a training-free, representation-level, state-directed write recovers much of the gap (sim 3–26% → 47–65%; real xArm pooled 14–43% → 83–100%, with only 5 trials per cell). For our G1 benchmark it is the closest precedent for **timing as a controlled variable** and for a clean **switch-success metric with Reach/Fin|R decomposition**. It covers **no** speech, overlap, human or motion-quality aspects, and it releases no code that I could find. It also corrects our novelty note: the paired with/without-event protocol is not in Causeway. Whoever designs the G1 protocol and metrics should read §III-C/D, §V-A and Table II.
